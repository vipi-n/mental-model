# Stock Mental Models

## Purpose

Produce a decision-ready public-equity analysis using the full mental-model latticework, not a single ratio or narrative. Separate business quality from ticker valuation, apply governance and solvency vetoes before price frameworks, and state what evidence would change the decision.

## Required reference

Read [references/stock_mental_models.md](references/stock_mental_models.md) completely before performing the first analysis in a task. Use its named-model inventory and research sequence as the controlling framework. Re-read the relevant sector and model sections when evidence raises a specific issue.

Treat the Reddit-derived material as a framework and source register, not as verified company evidence or an endorsement of its stock calls, forecasts, or historical price bands.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. Research current facts. Record the price, date, reporting period, and source cutoff.
2. Prefer exchange filings, annual reports, quarterly results, transcripts, credit-rating reports, regulator data, and official shareholding disclosures. Use aggregators only as cross-checks.
3. Distinguish reported fact, management claim, framework-author claim, and analyst inference.
4. Test every model in the reference. Mark it `positive`, `negative`, `mixed`, `inconclusive`, or `not applicable`; give a reason for every material classification.
5. Apply industry-quality, governance, cash-conversion, dilution, pledge, and solvency gates before valuation or Phoenix/Dragon levels.
6. Never infer that a falling price is automatically an opportunity or that a low P/E is automatically cheap.
7. Never issue an entry range from technical levels alone. Tie each tranche to both valuation and fundamental evidence.
8. Do not fabricate missing metrics. State the gap, its decision impact, and the evidence needed.
9. Show calculations and assumptions sufficiently for another analyst to reproduce the conclusion.
10. Give a direct action. Do not hide behind a generic disclaimer, but express uncertainty and conditions honestly.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the decision

Capture or reasonably infer:

- Company, ticker, exchange, current price, and price date.
- Initiate, add, hold, trim, sell, or reject question.
- Holding period and required return.
- Existing quantity, average cost, and position size when supplied.
- Maximum intended allocation and relevant portfolio concentration when supplied.

Do not let the user's purchase price determine business value. Use it only for tax, liquidity, portfolio, and implementation considerations. If portfolio details are absent, analyze the security and label sizing guidance as provisional rather than blocking the work.

### 2. Assemble an evidence pack

Gather the smallest sufficient current source set:

- Five to ten years of annual financial history when available.
- Latest annual report and latest quarterly filing.
- Latest investor presentation and earnings-call transcript.
- Exchange announcements and credit-rating reports.
- Shareholding, promoter pledge, insider transactions, warrants, ESOPs, and dilution.
- Material subsidiaries, related parties, contingent liabilities, auditor remarks, and capital commitments.
- Relevant competitors, scaled industry leader, and regulator or industry data.

For time-sensitive work, browse the web and cite direct links. Reconcile conflicting figures by definition, period, consolidation level, and source quality. State unresolved conflicts.

### 3. Map the business before the ticker

Explain concisely:

- Economic pool and who captures industry value.
- Products, customers, revenue mechanism, geography, concentration, and cyclicality.
- Business architecture and unit economics.
- Life-cycle stage and reinvestment runway.
- Owned bottlenecks, constraints, dependencies, and failure points.
- Appropriate archetype: toll booth, capillary, shared-scale flywheel, turnover machine, restaurant/commodity, treadmill, or another reference archetype.

Identify whether growth improves per-share economics or merely increases activity, assets, employees, acquisitions, or order book.

### 4. Run the full model latticework

Create a model-coverage table with:

`model or cluster | applicability | evidence | finding | decision impact`

At minimum, explicitly test these clusters:

- Foundational high-quality checklist and corporate life cycle.
- Economic pool, business architecture, moat, Maginot, Caged Bird, Mangrove/Marigold, Lindy, bottleneck, and constraint removal.
- Margin stack, shared scale, pricing power, asset turns, operating leverage, and low-margin species.
- Capital cycle, competitive intensity, reverse engineering/Gorilla/5L, and opportunity cost.
- Janitor Economy, Red Queen, Winner's Curse, Hamster Wheel, Oxygen Test, Cockroach Theory, and Toxic Pond.
- ROCE/ROIC, incremental ROIC, capital allocation, leverage, pledge, acquisitions, buybacks, dilution, incentives, and governance-forensic models.
- Cash conversion, FCF, depreciation/capex, working capital, cash verification, accounting quality, and guidance/foreshock models.
- Two-Engine valuation, expectation gap, mean/median P/E trap, re-rating/de-rating, compression, and business-state versus ticker-state.
- Phoenix Forge, Dragon Flight, resilience, behavioral risks, and staged allocation.
- Every applicable sector overlay in the reference.

Do not force irrelevant models. A clear `not applicable—because ...` is valid. Investigate convergence when several independent warnings point to the same underlying weakness; do not double-count correlated symptoms as separate facts.

### 5. Normalize financial quality

Calculate or extract, using consistent consolidated definitions where possible:

- Revenue, EBITDA/EBIT, PAT, EPS, and per-share FCF growth.
- Gross, operating, and net-margin direction.
- ROCE/ROIC and incremental ROIC versus an explicit cost-of-capital assumption.
- CFO/PAT, cumulative CFO versus cumulative PAT, and free cash flow.
- Receivable, inventory, payable, and cash-conversion-cycle trends.
- Gross debt, net debt, leases, debt/equity, interest coverage, and maturity/refinancing risk.
- Capital expenditure, CWIP, utilization, depreciation, and mature-cohort returns.
- Share-count growth, warrants, ESOPs, convertibles, promoter holding, and pledge.
- Other income, capitalized costs, exceptional items, related-party movements, and auditor qualifications.

Explain sector-specific distortions rather than applying industrial-company ratios mechanically. For banks and NBFCs, use lender economics and asset-quality measures; for EPC/infrastructure, separate order-book, execution, policy, and financing cycles; use the other sector overlays when applicable.

### 6. Apply vetoes before valuation

Treat these as potential overrides to an apparently cheap price:

- Evidence of fraud, material accounting irregularity, or unreliable disclosure.
- Unfunded liquidity risk, covenant stress, or plausible permanent impairment.
- Promoter pledge or dilution that can create forced selling or transfer value.
- Persistent CFO/PAT weakness without a credible, temporary explanation.
- Incremental returns below the cost of capital while reinvestment accelerates.
- Structurally toxic industry economics or a broken moat.

State whether a veto is active, watchlisted, cleared, or unresolvable. Phoenix/Dragon is an allocation overlay only after the quality and solvency gate passes.

### 7. Value both engines

Separate:

1. Growth in normalized per-share earnings or FCF.
2. Change in the defensible valuation multiple.

Build bear, base, and bull scenarios over an explicit horizon. For each, show starting normalized metric, growth, dilution, terminal metric/multiple, estimated value, expected CAGR, and key assumptions. Use DCF, residual-income, sum-of-parts, or sector-specific valuation when P/E is unsuitable.

Also calculate:

- What expectations the current price appears to embed.
- A price that meets the required base-case return.
- Downside under a realistic adverse case, not merely the 52-week low.
- Sensitivity to dilution, leverage, margins, and multiple compression when material.

Historical average multiples are evidence, not an anchor. Adjust for changes in moat, returns, growth, accounting quality, capital intensity, and rates.

### 8. Decide and implement

Choose one primary action:

- `ADD`: quality and risk gates pass, expected return is attractive, and allocation has room.
- `HOLD`: thesis remains intact but valuation, evidence, or sizing does not justify more.
- `TRIM`: thesis may remain viable but valuation, concentration, or risk/reward has worsened.
- `SELL`: thesis is falsified, a veto is active, or opportunity cost dominates.
- `REJECT/WATCH`: no position should be initiated until specified evidence or valuation appears.

If conditions genuinely split the answer, give one primary action followed by a precise exception, such as `HOLD small; SELL if position exceeds X%` or `NO ADD until pledge falls below Y%`.

Create an accumulation or reduction ladder only when requested or decision-useful. Express each tranche as a percentage of intended final position and attach fundamental prerequisites. Do not imply false precision.

### 9. Pre-write monitoring and exit rules

List measurable:

- Thesis-confirming evidence.
- Disconfirming evidence.
- Add triggers.
- Trim or sell triggers.
- Next filing/event and review date.

Prefer business and balance-sheet triggers over arbitrary price stop-losses for long-horizon investing. Include price only when it changes expected return, position risk, or market-based financing risk.

## Required output

Lead with the decision and confidence. Then provide:

1. **Decision snapshot** — action, current price/date, quality score, valuation view, and one-sentence reason.
2. **Thesis** — why the business can or cannot compound per-share value.
3. **Model coverage** — full applicability table, grouped compactly without omitting negative or inapplicable models.
4. **Financial and forensic evidence** — trends, cash conversion, balance sheet, dilution, governance, and source quality.
5. **Valuation** — transparent bear/base/bull math, expected return, and what is priced in.
6. **Action plan** — add/hold/trim/sell/reject, provisional position sizing, and conditional entry or exit ladder.
7. **Monitoring** — proof points, kill triggers, and next review trigger.
8. **Sources** — direct links near the claims they support, prioritizing primary evidence.

End with exactly these six labeled lines:

- **Business quality:**
- **Valuation:**
- **Thesis:**
- **Disconfirming evidence:**
- **Action:**
- **Next review trigger:**

## Quality check before delivery

Confirm that:

- The reference was read completely.
- Every model cluster was tested and sector overlays were handled.
- Current primary sources support the material facts.
- Per-share cash and incremental returns were emphasized over reported growth.
- Governance and solvency gates preceded valuation and allocation frameworks.
- Scenario math reconciles and dilution is included.
- The action is direct, conditional where necessary, and consistent with the evidence.
- The user can tell exactly what would make the conclusion change.
